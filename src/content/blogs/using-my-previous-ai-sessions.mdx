**My own AI sessions are a valuable resource.** If your conversations aren't saved locally, consider changing that so your agent can reuse them.

## Backtesting

- **Try new instructions on old sessions.** A new prompt, `AGENTS.md` rule, or skill, tested where I actually wanted it to apply.
- **Check what the model does.** Does it discover the skill? Do the instructions work?
- **Use my real scenarios.** My actual situations are better tests for my setup than scenarios AI invents.

## Instructions

- **Be careful letting AI write prompts for AI.** A fish can't teach you how to fish. I feel like that applies here.
- **Treat instructions as a holy source.** Verbose, messy slop gets fed into every session. I feel like the model picks up that style.
- **Use a quality filter.** `designing-agent-instructions`, read whenever my instructions change. I'd like to share this skill.

## Setup

- **Pi and Codex share everything.** Synced chats, the same `AGENTS.md` and skills.
- **Voice matters.** I use Codex more because its real-time voice integration on iOS is really good.
- **Passive skills use `-ing` names.** The agent discovers them when needed: `managing-secrets` (1Password), `managing-health` (Hevy and medication), `managing-finances`, `managing-mail`, `managing-tasks`.
- **Active prompts use action names.** Things I invoke, like `/visualize` and `/simplify`.

## Other uses

- **Check my impressions.** Was Pi becoming less concise? Was the conversion extension changing my system prompt? Check old sessions.
- **Pick up unfinished work.** Recover earlier decisions, like the timestamps and cuts for my talk edit.
- **Remember forgotten ideas.** Ask the agent to find them. Even this list draws on sessions I'd forgotten about.

## Writing

- **Capture ideas before I lose them.** Notes in my wording, labeled AI-generated. Refine into a full post later. I review before publication.
